# Compliance duties of a large multi-site manufacturer

Source notes for `reports/RegTech market and gaps.md`: the laws and standards a big multi-site manufacturer must meet (Australia first, then EU and US), the evidence each needs, the software used and where it breaks. Research 4 Nov 2026 by Claude; needs human review.

**How sure:** items without a tag were checked on 4 Nov 2026, mostly on law-firm or vendor pages, not regulators. **[K]** = from memory, not re-checked; the link is the rule's official home. Check [K] items before quoting them to a customer. A Safe Work Australia page timed out, so WHS details are mostly [K]. Status words: **IN FORCE**, **PHASING IN** (dates still ahead), **PROPOSED** (draft or policy), **CHANGED** (cut back or delayed).

Frame: six families of duty (safety, cyber, environment and climate, people and privacy, product and quality, corporate). Almost every duty means **a register** (what you have), **a record** (what you did) and **a report** (on a schedule or after an event). Evidence is what an auditor or regulator asks to see.

## 1. Cyber and IT

Bottom line: for an Australian manufacturer the cyber duties that truly bind are the **ransomware payment report** and the Privacy Act's breach rules. **SOCI** binds only owners of listed critical assets (e.g. food and grocery distribution, energy, liquid fuels, declared defence industry assets). ISO 27001, Essential Eight, IEC 62443 and NIST CSF are voluntary unless customers, insurers, boards or SOCI ask. Firms selling into the EU face **NIS2** and the **Cyber Resilience Act**.

**Ransomware payment reports (Cyber Security Act 2024, Part 3): IN FORCE**
- Who: a business in Australia with turnover over A$3m (or that owns critical infrastructure) that pays a ransom, or has one paid for it. Report to the Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) within 72 hours of paying. — [PIP](https://pip.com.au/cyber-security/cyber-security-act-2024/); [Ai Group](https://www.australianindustrygroup.com.au/news/blogs/2025/mandatory-ransomware-payment-reporting/)
- Started 30 May 2025; 2025 was education-first; enforced from 1 Jan 2026. — [Snare](https://www.snaresolutions.com/australias-ransomware-reporting-regime/); [Key IT](https://keyit.com.au/news/ransomware-reporting-enforcement-2026/)
- Penalty disputed: 60 or 300 penalty units (about A$99,000). Check the Act. — [Dundas Lawyers](https://www.dundaslawyers.com.au/ransomware-payment-reporting-obligations/); [Cliffside](https://www.cliffside.com.au/insights/cyber-security-act-2024-australia/)
- [K] The same Act added a "limited use" rule (info given to ASD or the National Cyber Security Coordinator can't be used against the reporter for most purposes), a Cyber Incident Review Board, and security standards for smart devices. — [legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/)

**SOCI Act 2018 (Security of Critical Infrastructure): IN FORCE**
- Risk management program rules (CIRMP) began 17 Feb 2023 and cover 13 asset classes, including food and grocery. — [Lander & Rogers](https://landers.com.au/legal-insights-news/risk-management-program-PSO-switched-on); [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/resilient-infrastructure-risk-management-program-rules-under-soci-act-now-in-force/)
- Food and grocery is narrow: distribution networks of critical supermarket retailers and food or grocery wholesalers. Making food alone does not count. — [CISC](https://www.cisc.gov.au:443/information-for-your-industry/food-and-grocery/legislation-regulation-and-compliance/soci-act-2018)
- A covered entity must adopt, keep and follow a written CIRMP, review it, and lodge a **yearly board-approved report** (a statement, not the full program). The 2022 penalty was 150 penalty units (about A$166,500 for a company then); check today's value. — [Lander & Rogers](https://landers.com.au/legal-insights-news/risk-management-program-PSO-switched-on); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/au/en/insights/risk-regulation/critical-infrastructure-reforms.html); [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/infrastructure-update-new-obligations-under-the-security-of-critical-infrastructure-act-2018-cth-september-2022/)
- [K] Also: register asset owners and operating details; report cyber incidents within 12 hours (significant impact) or 72 hours (relevant impact); align the CIRMP cyber part to a recognised framework (ISO 27001, Essential Eight Maturity Level 1, NIST CSF, C2M2 or AESCSF) by Aug 2024; yearly report due within 90 days after the Australian financial year ends. Run by the Cyber and Infrastructure Security Centre (Home Affairs). — [CISC](https://www.cisc.gov.au/)
- Sectors covered and SOCI vs IEC 62443: gaps file Q5. Physical and personnel hazards: section 6.
- Australian vendors already sell "SOCI CIRMP readiness" (e.g. Protecht). — [Protecht](https://www.protechtgroup.com/en-au/blog/soci-cirmp-deadlines-are-looming-are-you-ready)

**Frameworks (voluntary unless a contract, regulator or SOCI requires them)**
- [K] **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**: certifiable security management system. Annex A has 93 controls in 4 themes (organisational, people, physical, technological). 3-year certificate with yearly surveillance audits. Move from the 2013 version was due 31 Oct 2025. — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)
- [K] **Essential Eight** (ASD): application control, patch apps, Office macro settings, user app hardening, restrict admin rights, patch operating systems, MFA, regular backups; each scored Maturity Level 0-3. Mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities (under the PSPF); voluntary for companies, but widely used by them and insurers as a benchmark; a SOCI-accepted framework. **Intune and Defender data maps straight to several controls** (patch level, macros, MFA, admin rights). — [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/); [Essential Eight page](https://www.cyber.gov.au/resources-business-and-government/essential-cyber-security/essential-eight)
- [K] **IEC 62443** (plant/OT networks): part 2-1 the owner's security program; 3-2 zones and conduits (network segments); 3-3 security levels SL1-SL4; 4-2 components. OT tools (Claroty, Nozomi, Dragos) produce asset lists and segmentation evidence. — [ISA](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-iec-62443-series-of-standards)
- [K] **NIST CSF 2.0**: out 26 Feb 2024; added a sixth function, "Govern". Common board-reporting language for US multinationals. — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
- [K] **SOC 2**: AICPA attestation (Type I = design; Type II = working over a period), mainly for service providers. Manufacturers usually ask their SaaS vendors for one, so it is a supplier-risk item. — [AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/)

**EU**
- **NIS2** (Directive 2022/2555): IN FORCE, national laws patchy. [K] Due in national law by 17 Oct 2024 (many states late); 18 sectors; managers personally accountable. Manufacturing is an Annex II sector (medical devices, computers and electronics, electrical equipment, machinery, motor vehicles, other transport); food and chemicals are also in scope. Large Annex I entities are "essential"; Annex II entities are "important", checked after the fact, with lower top fines. — [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/nis2-essential-important-entities.html); [Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/?p=235075)
- NIS2 incident reports: early warning within 24 hours, notice within 72 hours, final report within one month. Managers can be personally liable. — [OpenStatus](https://www.openstatus.dev/guides/nis2-incident-reporting-requirements); [Taylor Wessing](https://digital-regulation.taylorwessing.com/welcome/the-second-network-and-information-security-directive-act-nis2)
- [K] NIS2 top fines: essential €10m or 2% of world turnover; important €7m or 1.4%. — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj)
- [K] **Cyber Resilience Act**: makers of products with digital parts sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities from 11 Sep 2026; full duties from 11 Dec 2027. Matters for connected equipment. PHASING IN. — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj)

**US**
- [K] SEC cyber rules (adopted Jul 2023, in effect Dec 2023): listed firms disclose material incidents on Form 8-K Item 1.05 within 4 business days of deciding an incident is material, and describe cyber risk management yearly in the 10-K (Item 1C). Not checked whether the SEC rolled these back in 2025-26. — [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/); [SEC press release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-139)
- [K] Defence suppliers: CMMC certification, phased in from Nov 2025.

Our reading: the easiest cyber evidence to automate is device posture (Intune), MFA, admin rights, patch age, backups and access reviews: most of Essential Eight ML1-ML2 and much of ISO 27001's technological controls. A hub that sees Intune and identity can show **Essential Eight maturity per site** live, not as a yearly consultant report. The ransomware 72-hour and NIS2 24-hour clocks are event deadlines; an incident with a countdown and a pre-filled report fits. OT is usually the weak spot: engineering owns plant networks, and they are often left out of ISO 27001 scope.

Not checked: exact ransomware penalty; indexed SOCI penalties; which manufacturers hold SOCI assets (defence assets are declared privately, no public list); how Australian manufacturers score on Essential Eight.

## 2. Work health and safety (WHS)

Bottom line: the heaviest daily load. Mostly registers (chemicals, plant, licences, training), records (inspections, risk assessments, toolbox talks, consultation) and fast incident reports to a state regulator. **Every state now has psychosocial rules; Victoria was last (1 Dec 2025).** Industrial manslaughter can mean jail for officers.

- [K] **Structure:** the model WHS Act and Regulations apply everywhere except Victoria (own OHS Act 2004). Each state has its own regulator (SafeWork NSW, WorkSafe Victoria, WorkSafe Queensland and others) plus Comcare for Commonwealth workplaces. A multi-site manufacturer can answer to 5+ regulators. — [Safe Work Australia](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/)
- **Victoria psychosocial (IN FORCE 1 Dec 2025):** OHS (Psychological Health) Regulations 2025: identify, assess and control psychosocial hazards (bullying, overload, poor support) and review the controls. — [WorkSafe Victoria](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/news/2025-12/new-regulations-make-psychological-health-priority); [Clayton Utz](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2025/october/victorian-employers-get-new-requirements-on-psychosocial-hazards)
- [K] The model WHS Regulations added psychosocial duties from 1 Apr 2023 (NSW had a code earlier). — [SWA psychosocial](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/mental-health/psychosocial-hazards)
- **Industrial manslaughter:** Commonwealth offence from 1 Jul 2024; up to A$18m for a company and 25 years' jail for a person. Most states have their own. Victoria's first workplace manslaughter fine was raised to a record A$3m on appeal (reported Feb 2026). Victoria in 2025: A$17m+ in penalties and 137 prosecutions and enforceable undertakings. — [AICD](https://www.aicd.com.au/regulatory-compliance/the-psychosocial-regulatory-landscape.html); [Safety Solutions](https://www.safetysolutions.net.au/content/business/article/victoria-in-2025-17m-in-penalties-and-137-prosecutions-and-enforceable-undertakings-987785989)
- [K] **Notifiable incidents** (model WHS Act ss 35-39): a death, serious injury or illness, or "dangerous incident" (chemical release, fire, plant collapse, electric shock) must be reported **immediately** by the fastest means; written notice within 48 hours if asked. Leave the site untouched except to help people or make it safe. Keep the record 5 years. Not reporting is an offence. — [SWA notifiable incidents](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/notifiable-incidents)
- [K] **Hazardous chemicals** (Part 7.1): a register with current Safety Data Sheets (SDS); a manifest to the regulator and placards above set amounts; GHS labels and SDS (Revision 7 mandatory from 1 Jan 2026); exposure standards are becoming "workplace exposure limits". Engineered stone banned since 1 Jul 2024. Chemwatch and ChemAlert are widely used. — [SWA chemicals](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/chemicals)
- [K] **Plant** (Schedule 5): some designs (pressure equipment, cranes, lifts) and some items (boilers, pressure vessels, larger cranes, lifts) must be registered and renewed. All plant needs inspection and maintenance records; lock-out/tag-out and guarding are standard audit items. — [SWA plant](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/plant-and-structures)
- [K] **High risk work licences** (Schedule 3): forklift (LF), order-picking forklift (LO), cranes, dogging, rigging, scaffolding, pressure equipment and more. Issued by states, valid 5 years; the employer must check them and keep proof. Site "tickets", inductions and contractor qualifications sit on top. — [SWA licences](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/licences-and-registration)
- [K] **ISO 45001:2018** (amended 2024 for climate): certifiable safety management system; 3-year certificate, yearly surveillance audit; voluntary but often needed in tenders. — [ISO 45001](https://www.iso.org/standard/63787.html)

| Duty | Evidence | How often | Typical tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report serious incidents fast | Notice, investigation, site-preservation note | Immediately; keep 5 years | EHS suite, SafetyCulture/Mitti, phone plus spreadsheet |
| Manage psychosocial risk | Hazard register, consultation, surveys, control reviews | Ongoing; after changes or complaints | EHS suite, HR system, survey tools |
| Chemical register | Register, SDS under 5 years old, risk assessments, manifest | Ongoing | Chemwatch, ChemAlert, EHS suite |
| Plant | Registration certificates, inspection and maintenance logs | Yearly renewals; scheduled inspections | CMMS (SAP PM, Maximo), EHS suite |
| Licences and tickets | Licence copies with expiry dates, training matrix | Renew every 5 years; site-specific | LMS, contractor tools (Rapid Global, Cm3, Avetta), spreadsheets |
| ISO 45001 | System documents, internal audits, management review | Yearly surveillance audit | Document control, EHS suite |

Our reading: licences join to rosters: an unlicensed person rostered on a forklift shift can be caught **before** the shift, and access control can refuse a zone when a ticket lapses. Few EHS tools join rosters, access logs and licence registers. (Gallagher already blocks doors on lapsed tickets: physical file section 5.) Psychosocial hazards (overwork, fatigue) can be partly shown from roster and overtime data, with care for surveillance and privacy law (section 4).

Not checked: indexed WHS penalties per state; share of Australian manufacturers running WHS on spreadsheets vs a platform.

## 3. Environment and climate

Bottom line: climate reporting is the fastest-growing duty. **Group 2 (most big manufacturers) began its first AASB S2 year on 1 Jul 2026**; first reports land late 2027. NGER and the Safeguard Mechanism already apply to heavy emitters. The EU cut CSRD to firms with **more than 1,000 staff**; CBAM's main phase began 1 Jan 2026.

**ASRS / AASB S2 mandatory climate reporting (Corporations Act Ch 2M): PHASING IN**
- **Group 1:** years from 1 Jan 2025. [K] Two of: revenue A$500m+, assets A$1bn+, 500+ staff; or large NGER reporters.
- **Group 2:** years starting on or after 1 Jul 2026. Two of three: revenue A$200m+, gross assets A$500m+, 250+ staff; plus NGER reporters and asset owners with A$5bn+. For a 30 June year end, the first report covers the year to 30 Jun 2027 and is lodged around Oct 2027. Sources differ on threshold wording and on BN Law's "31 October 2026" lodgement date; one Eco-Shaper page gives Group 1-style thresholds for Group 2 (likely wrong). — [Anthesis guide](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/au/insights/asrs-and-aasb-s2-a-guide-to-mandatory-climate-reporting-in-australia/); [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/au/regulations/asrs-aasb-s2/); [Rulesmate](https://rulesmate.com.au/insights/asrs-group-2-climate-reports-1-july-2026); [Carbon Impact HQ](https://carbonimpacthq.com/journal/australia-aasb-s2-group-2-climate-reporting-2026); [Sustainability Tracker](https://www.sustainabilitytracker.com/article/aasb-s2-group-2-climate-reporting/); [BN Law](https://bnlaw.com.au/knowledge-hub/insights/mandatory-climate-reporting-keep-your-disclosures-crystal-clear); [AASB FAQ](https://aasb.gov.au/research-resources/knowledge-hub/aasb-s2-knowledge-hub/aasb-s2-frequently-asked-questions/general-faqs/); [Eco-Shaper Group 2](https://eco-shaper.com/aasb-s2-group-2-mandatory-climate-reporting-australia-2026/); [Eco-Shaper 5 ways (conflicting)](https://eco-shaper.com/aasb-s2-reporting-requirements-5-ways-to-get-ready/)
- **Group 3:** from 1 Jul 2027. [K] Revenue A$50m, assets A$25m, 100 staff. One source says these are under review (not confirmed). — [Carbon Halo](https://carbonhalo.com/glossary/aasb-s2-effective-date/)
- **Scope 1 and 2 from year one** (collect data from 1 Jul 2026); **Scope 3 from year two**. Limited assurance from the first period; [K] rising to reasonable assurance by about 2030. ASIC enforces (RG 280) and says year one will be handled pragmatically, which is not a pause. — [Rulesmate](https://rulesmate.com.au/insights/asrs-group-2-climate-reports-1-july-2026); [Spectre](https://www.spectreco.com/blog/what-aasb-s2-means-for-your-business-before-july-2026); [Naturaliste](https://naturalistesolutions.com.au/insights/aasb-s2-group-2-climate-reporting/); [SAI Assurance](https://saiassurance.com.au/news-resources/blog/aasb-s2-reporting-starts-1-july-2026-is-your-organisation-ready/)
- [K] Directors sign a declaration. "Modified liability" protects Scope 3, scenario analysis and transition plans in the first years. — [ASIC RG 280](https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/sustainability-reporting/)
- Supplier data requests and vendors: gaps file Q3.

**NGER (National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting): IN FORCE**
- Facility threshold: 25 kt CO2-e (Scope 1 + 2) or more. — [NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Climate-change/guiding-principles-alignment-national-greenhouse-and-energy-reporting-scheme-safeguard-mechanism)
- [K] Group thresholds 50 kt CO2-e or 200 TJ energy; facility energy 100 TJ. Report to the Clean Energy Regulator **by 31 October** each year for the previous financial year (31 Oct partly checked). — [Clean Energy Regulator](https://cer.gov.au/)

**Safeguard Mechanism: IN FORCE**
- Covers facilities emitting over 100,000 t CO2-e (Scope 1) a year. Baselines fall 4.9% a year to 2030, then an indicative 3.285% a year; the 2031-35 rate is set in the 2026-27 review. Facilities over baseline must hand in ACCUs or Safeguard credits. — [CER insights](https://cer.gov.au/document/2024-25-safeguard-mechanism-data-insights); [Carbon Market Institute](https://carbonmarketinstitute.org/2026/04/16/safeguard-mechanism-performance-promising-as-industry-prepares-for-critical-review/)

**EPA licences and pollution reports: IN FORCE (per state)**
- [K] State EPAs license each site (e.g. NSW Environment Protection Licence under the POEO Act, with a yearly return and monitoring data; Victoria's EP Act 2017 permissions plus a "general environmental duty" since 1 Jul 2021). National Pollutant Inventory (NPI) reports are due yearly by 30 Sep above substance thresholds. Waste tracking and trade waste agreements too. — [NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/); [EPA Victoria](https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/); [NPI](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/npi)
- [K] **ISO 14001:2015**: certifiable environmental system; a revision is expected around 2026 (check). — [ISO 14001](https://www.iso.org/standard/60857.html)

**EU CBAM (carbon border tax): PHASING IN**
- Main phase began 1 Jan 2026. The exemption is now **50 t of CBAM goods per importer per year** (replacing the old €150 per shipment; electricity and hydrogen excluded); once past 50 t, the whole year counts. Certificate sales for 2026 imports start 1 Feb 2027. First declaration due 31 Aug or 30 Sep 2027 (sources differ). — [Customs Support](https://www.customssupport.com/cbam-omnibus-i-package/); [CBAM Guide](https://cbamguide.com/compliance/de-minimis/); [Substantio](https://substantio.com/cbam-eu-omnibus-package/)
- [K] Goods: iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, electricity. The EU importer pays, but Australian steel and aluminium exporters get asked for plant-level emissions data. Extending CBAM to downstream goods is in final EU talks after a Parliament vote on 15 Sep 2026: PROPOSED. — [Substantio](https://substantio.com/cbam-eu-omnibus-package/)

**EU CSRD / CSDDD after "Omnibus I": CHANGED**
- Provisional deal Dec 2025; Directive (EU) 2026/470 in force 18 Mar 2026. CSRD now covers firms with over 1,000 staff and turnover above €450m (one source adds "or balance sheet above €25m"). First reports cover FY2027 (years from 1 Jan 2027), due 2028. CSDDD (supply-chain due diligence) now covers over 5,000 staff and €1.5bn turnover. — [Tracera](https://tracera.com/eu-omnibus-simplification-package/); [Normative](https://normative.io/insight/the-omnibus-simplification-package-explained/); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/the-omnibus-reaches-its-destination-csrd-and-cs3d-20/); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/articles/eu-council-and-eu-parliament-reach-an-agreement-to-simplify-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/); [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/eu-parliament-approves-omnibus-agreement-to-cut-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/)
- [K] Non-EU groups (e.g. an Australian parent) come in through EU turnover or EU subsidiaries; check the final text.

**US**
- [K] The SEC climate rule is effectively dead (the SEC stopped defending it in 2025). California SB 253 (emissions) and SB 261 (climate risk) apply to big firms doing business in California; SB 261 was paused by courts in late 2025. Check both. — [CARB](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/)

Our reading: Scope 1 and 2 data comes from energy bills, fuel and refrigerant records, which sit in finance systems (Xero, MYOB, ERP invoices) and site meters. A finance connector that tags energy and fuel spend by site is a cheap start. The directors' declaration makes a "where each number came from" view valuable. NGER, Safeguard, NPI and AASB S2 all roll site data up to group level: the same multi-site problem as WHS.

Not checked: CSRD rules for non-EU parents; California status in Nov 2026; ISO 14001 and 9001 revisions; EU Deforestation Regulation date (delayed again).

## 4. People and privacy

Bottom line: Privacy Act **Tranche 1 is law (Dec 2024)**; its automated-decision rule applies from **10 Dec 2026** (details: gaps file Q10). **Tranche 2 is only a draft (31 Aug 2026)** and keeps the employee records exemption. Workplace surveillance is regulated state by state. Wage underpayment became a crime on 1 Jan 2025. The modern slavery threshold stays at A$100m.

**Privacy**
- **Tranche 1** (Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024): a legal right to sue for serious invasions of privacy ([K] from Jun 2025); automated-decision transparency in privacy policies by 10 Dec 2026; a mandate for a Children's Online Privacy Code. — [Wotton Kearney, 2024 (PDF)](https://www.wottonkearney.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/What-You-Need-to-Know-%E2%80%93-The-Privacy-and-Other-Legislation-Amendment-Bill-2024.pdf); [LK Law](https://www.lk.law/2026/09/australias-privacy-act-reforms-inside-the-second-tranche/)
- [K] Tranche 1 also brought tiered civil penalties, infringement notices and stronger OAIC powers. Top penalty for serious interference: the greatest of A$50m, 3x the benefit, or 30% of adjusted turnover. Notifiable Data Breaches: assess a suspected breach within 30 days; tell the OAIC and affected people as soon as practicable. — [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/)
- **Children's Online Privacy Code:** OAIC draft out 31 Mar 2026 (feedback to 5 Jun 2026); must be final by 10 Dec 2026. — [G+T](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/exposure-draft-of-childrens-online-privacy-code-released); [OAIC blog](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/blog/better-privacy-protections-for-children-are-coming)
- **Tranche 2:** draft Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026, released 31 Aug 2026 with a consultation paper and about 40 proposals. Submissions closed 18 Sep 2026. The Government plans to introduce it before the end of 2026; no start date. Expected: a "fair and reasonable" test for handling personal information, tiered penalties, infringement notices. PROPOSED. — [McCullough Robertson](https://mccullough.com.au/2026/08/31/privacy-act-reforms-tranche-2-proposals/); [Gadens](https://www.gadens.com/insights/tranche-2-privacy-reforms-key-changes); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/draft-tranche-2-privacy-reforms-what-they-mean-for-businesses); [Colin Biggers & Paisley](https://www.cbp.com.au/insights/publications/privacy-amendment-(personal-data-protection)-bill-2026-exposure-draft-released-for-second-tranche-o); [Wotton Kearney, Sep 2026 (PDF)](https://www.wottonkearney.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Tranche-2-privacy-reforms-Wotton-Kearney.pdf); [Ashurst](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/data-bytes-au-key-australian-privacy-updates-so-far-in-2026/)
- The draft does **not** remove the small-business or **employee records** exemptions, and leaves out a right to erasure, mandatory privacy impact assessments and a direct right to sue. — [Russell Kennedy](https://www.russellkennedy.com.au/insights-events/insights/privacy-reform-tranche-2-arrives-major-changes-proposed-key-reforms-still-missing); [Corrs](https://www.corrs.com.au/insights/australias-privacy-act-reforms-fundamental-changes-proposed-in-new-exposure-draft)
- Plain words: records an employer keeps about its employees are still mostly outside the Privacy Act. **CCTV footage, contractor and visitor access logs, and biometrics are not employee records**, so they are covered.

**Workplace surveillance**
- [K] **NSW** (Workplace Surveillance Act 2005): 14 days' written notice before camera, computer or tracking surveillance; cameras visible with signs; a written policy for computer surveillance; covert surveillance needs a magistrate. **ACT** (Workplace Privacy Act 2011) is similar and requires consultation. IN FORCE. — [NSW legislation](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2005-047)
- **Victoria:** no dedicated Act. The Surveillance Devices Act 1999 Part 2A bans workplace cameras and listening devices in toilets, washrooms, change rooms and lactation rooms. Labor's PROPOSED package, if re-elected on 28 Nov 2026: notice and consultation before new monitoring; biometrics only when nothing less intrusive works; limits on emotion recognition; human review of significant automated decisions; worker access to data used against them; a ban on selling surveillance data. It follows a May 2025 parliamentary inquiry. — [Lander & Rogers](https://landers.com.au/legal-insights-news/vic-government-foreshadows-tougher-workplace-surveillance-laws); [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/vic-labor-moots-workplace-ai-and-biometrics-surveillance-curbs-627502); [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/victoria-moves-to-curb-workplace-ai-and-biometric-surveillance)

**Fair Work and awards: IN FORCE**
- [K] Deliberate underpayment of wages is a crime from 1 Jan 2025 (Closing Loopholes). Keep employee records 7 years; issue pay slips within 1 working day of payday. Where it breaks (our reading):
1. **One duty, many owners.** A forklift ticket is safety's duty, HR's record, security's gate check and operations' roster. Nobody sees all four, so someone with an expired ticket gets rostered.
2. **Site files, group reports.** NGER, AASB S2, modern slavery and SOCI are signed at group or board level, but data arrives from sites in different formats and is rolled up in spreadsheets once a year under deadline.
3. **Different regulators per state.** WHS notices, surveillance notice and EPA licences differ in NSW, Victoria and Queensland, so "the same" process splits at each border.
4. **Evidence is a screenshot.** Audits mostly get manual exports. Systems that already emit the data (Intune, access control, CCTV health, identity) are rarely wired up as continuous evidence.
5. **Short event clocks spread across teams:** WHS immediately, product injury 2 days, ransomware 72 hours, SOCI 12/72 hours, NIS2 24 hours, privacy breach 30-day assessment. One event (e.g. a cyber attack that stops a plant and hurts someone) can start several at once.
6. **Gap for a hub:** a duty-to-evidence map keyed by site and legal entity that pulls live evidence from connectors (Intune, access control, CCTV, rosters, finance) and leaves deep workflows to existing EHS and GRC tools. Deciding which law applies stays with a person (AI suggests, people approve).
